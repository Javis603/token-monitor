# Quota history

The desktop Limits view keeps an account’s quota windows together. Briefly hover an account to open a compact, non-activating preview beside the widget. Settings → Main Screen → Limits contains the history-card preference and can select automatic hover, click-only, or Off. Off dismisses any current or pending preview and disables both mouse and keyboard opening; historical observations continue to be recorded. With either opening mode enabled, clicking a non-control part of a row and Enter/Space work in either mode. The preview never takes keyboard or window focus, even on an explicit open or when changing the quota with the mouse. The source widget retains keyboard focus, including Escape to dismiss the preview.

This version shows current quota windows and their remaining/spend trend, plus a fixed summary of the last 14 local calendar days. It has no date input, earlier/later navigation or day/hour switch. A fixed-height reading below the chart shows today’s measured usage, or insufficient data when no interval was measured. Hovering a bar brightens only that bar and temporarily shows its date and amount in that reading; leaving the plot restores today and clears the highlight. The reading cannot browse to a historical date. Quota buttons change only the current quota being inspected. Detailed and daily observations continue to be stored for future history browsing; the storage/query layer's historical ranges are not exposed through preview IPC.

Moving between the account and preview, including their gap, keeps it visible. An untouched hover preview closes after leaving both; an explicit open remains until an outside click, Escape in the source widget, another explicit selection, or the owner hiding, moving, resizing or losing focus. Clicking the same explicitly opened account again closes it. Returning to the same account preserves its selected quota. Switching accounts or current quotas commits new content immediately, without retaining an outgoing picture. After the new content has painted and reported its measured size, the native card follows the same position/size spring as the sidebar cards. Rapid selections retarget from the current frame and velocity. Opening fades in new content over 150 ms; the frame settles over at most 650 ms. Background updates do not restart the motion, and reduced motion skips it. The reusable preview renderer loads alongside its first history query, and the successful initial payload is rendered without a duplicate query on page load. The panel follows the app’s native material, transparency, theme and font, including reduced-transparency and high-contrast preferences, without copying its custom background image. It flips at a screen edge and fits the current content, including when reopening an unchanged card at the default window bounds.

History accumulates from successful, non-stale readings already received by the desktop. Opening a panel never probes a provider. Previously cached readings retain their original observation time; failures do not become new measurements. Historical data from before observation started cannot be reconstructed. Remote quotas accumulate only while this desktop receives them; the history itself is local and is not uploaded to the Hub or iCloud.

A timely, consistent reading from another device continues the same account timeline. Repeated observations at the same timestamp do not add another sample or consumption, and older timestamps cannot replace newer readings. If devices disagree about the value or reset deadline, the affected interval is marked uncertain. The established observer then stays preferred while healthy; after it fails or exceeds the sampling gap, a replacement starts an uncertain baseline. After a disagreement, switching sources never creates a reset or a jump in measured consumption. Failures or missing readings from an inactive observer do not interrupt a healthy active one. Missing allowance metadata alone does not count as a disagreement; conflicting values or reset deadlines still do. Resets observed within one source still split the curve normally. These rules cannot detect a reset that occurred entirely between observations.

When the reset deadline and window duration are known (from explicit minutes, the daily/weekly kind, or known five-hour session semantics), the trend axis spans the complete advertised cycle even with late or no observations. Unobserved portions of the recorded curve remain blank; the first reading is placed at its actual observation time. Percentage quotas with known cycle bounds also show a faint dashed even-use guide, from 100% remaining at the cycle start to 0% at reset. Its accessible description identifies it as a reference rather than a forecast, without adding a visible caption to the compact card. It is independent of actual observations and is omitted for monetary units or unknown cycle bounds. Without those cycle bounds, the axis is explicitly labelled as the last 14 days and ends at the present; a future reset deadline is shown separately rather than implying a fabricated cycle start. The chart connects compatible observed values with straight segments, including across sampling gaps and consistent device handoffs. Drawing the line does not add intermediate observations. Compatible endpoints within one local day establish a net quota change for that day even across sampling gaps, including long gaps, or failed refreshes; hourly attribution requires both endpoints in the same local hour. Coverage remains partial. After a stale or failed source hands over, the replacement remains preferred while healthy; a later compatible handoff after failure or expiry still establishes same-bucket net change unless an actual disagreement was observed. Reset/refill boundaries, changed allowances, conflicting readings and confirmed missing-quota snapshots still break the curve. Failed refresh attempts alone do not cut compatible trend endpoints or discard their same-bucket net change. Trend and consumption comparisons tolerate up to one second of reset-deadline rounding drift, but neither connects observations across either reported deadline. Stored reset timestamps remain unchanged. When only the percentage or allowance temporarily becomes unavailable, the trend connects the valid readings on either side if their quota basis and cycle still match. No intermediate reading is invented. If only the allowance was missing and both valid percentage readings have the same quota basis and cycle, their net change counts in a daily bucket when both readings fall on that local calendar day. The hourly view applies the same rule within one local hour. Changes spanning buckets remain unallocated, and the interval retains partial coverage. Actual allowance changes, resets, refills, conflicts and confirmed missing-quota snapshots do not qualify. Daily quota usage is displayed as a share of the quota (for example, a change from 20% to 24% used displays as 4%). Internally it remains an observed percentage-point difference, rather than a relative growth rate. Consumption is an observed increase in used percentage, a decrease in a pure credits balance in its reported unit, or an increase in cumulative monetary spend. It is not token usage or an API-equivalent cost. A zero means no net change was observed; it does not prove there was no consumption, especially in a rolling window. Timing inside a sampling gap remains unknown. Daily net changes are saved with their collection date and read directly from those summaries, including after detailed observations expire; changing timezone does not move previously saved daily usage. Daily and hourly buckets use the desktop's local timezone, including distinct repeated hours when daylight saving time ends.

Quotas with a reported percentage or a known positive allowance use a fixed 0–100% remaining curve and consumption in percentage points, matching the account card. Command Code derives its monthly window duration from the billing API’s actual period start and end, including variable-length months. Missing or invalid cycle boundaries still use the explicitly labelled recent-range fallback. Monetary quotas also retain their original currency observations and show the current amount/allowance as supporting information. Each observation retains its allowance when known: changing, gaining or losing that denominator separates the observations without inventing consumption or a reset. Recovering the same denominator can establish a net change between compatible percentage endpoints under the bucket rule above. Historical percentage summaries are never rescaled to the current plan. Raw-only observations collected before an allowance or percentage was available remain raw-only; the app does not reconstruct old percentages from today's total. Pure wallets, explicitly unmetered top-ups and cumulative monetary spend keep their reported units. Display-only wallet estimates based on balance and month spend are not treated as measured quota percentages.

Consecutive unchanged readings are stored as one interval, even across hours or days. Each observed hour retains a small coverage summary: its first and last successful observations, sample count and maximum sampling gap. The unchanged value is stored only once for the whole interval. Value changes, reset/deadline changes, refresh-cadence changes and interruptions start another interval. Failed readings mark an interruption even if the next success arrives quickly. A successful provider snapshot also interrupts previously observed quotas from that active source when a quota is absent or has no usable reading. Returning readings after an interruption start a new stored interval; comparable endpoints across a sampling gap or failed refresh still establish their same-bucket net change. A label-only change retains history when the quota keeps its stable backend ID (`limitId`); without a stable ID, the label is part of its identity and a rename starts a separate history. A monetary reading with only its allowance missing follows the compatible-endpoint net-change rule above instead. The hourly view uses these coverage summaries; daily summaries are updated directly from the same successful observations. Both distinguish observed zero change from missing observations. Individual timestamps inside an unchanged interval are not retained. If a timezone change puts an old coverage summary across a new hour boundary, the view does not invent the original sample positions for those new hours.

Changes across time buckets, confirmed missing-quota snapshots or conflicting readings are reported separately, without inventing their distribution. Resets, refills, changing window deadlines and currency changes cannot be treated as ordinary consumption. In particular, rolling-window observations alone cannot separate new consumption from old usage expiring. Solid daily bars represent observed net quota changes, not a complete period total; missing intervals can hide consumption followed by a refill. Days without a measured interval stay blank rather than drawing a zero bar; a measured zero retains a small baseline mark. Coverage explanations remain accessible descriptions of the daily reading rather than permanent visible notes. Percentage charts label only the top and bottom ticks; daily charts omit scale labels and show exact amounts on hover, with accessible date and amount labels retained for every day.

Detailed observations cover the most recent 180 local calendar days, including today. Daily summaries cover the most recent 730 local calendar days. There are no per-quota or cross-account row caps: adding accounts does not shorten another account’s retention. Each successful reading updates its detailed interval and daily summary in one transaction before age-based cleanup. Summaries preserve observed consumption, sample counts, partial/unknown intervals, unallocated changes, reset counts and first/last observations. Old detail is removed at whole-day boundaries; an actual last reading may remain as a baseline for detecting the next gap or reset. Daily summaries retain the local date assigned when observations were collected; changing timezone does not reconstruct older daily boundaries. History follows provider, verified account or workspace, quota and balance currency across local, Hub and iCloud modes. Backend and reporting device identify the observation source, rather than starting another verified-account history. Reporting devices do not create separate histories for an identified account; their hashed identities are retained as observation provenance. Product variants with separate quota pools remain distinct. Anonymous or device-local account keys, including the unidentified Claude CLI placeholder and CLI rows showing only an organization label, remain scoped to their source. A verified Claude key without an email still shares history when falling back from OAuth to CLI; only a key derived from the CLI organization label is excluded, and different upstream account keys are not guessed to be equivalent from a matching display name, email or plan.

The private `limits-history.sqlite` database in application data contains hashed stream/account identities and numeric readings, without credentials or account email addresses. The desktop uses its bundled Node SQLite support on a dedicated worker thread. Writes update only the affected rows and queries read the selected date range and current quota cycle; the full history is not loaded into the main process. Committed writes are durable. Normal exit waits up to three seconds for queued writes, without cancelling an OS shutdown; a timeout is logged rather than holding the application open indefinitely. SQLite may create private `-wal` and `-shm` sidecars alongside the database. If the database cannot be opened, the desktop preserves it and keeps new observations in memory for the current run. Failed write transactions roll back both detail and summaries and can be retried by later observations. A persistence error in the panel asks the user to check free disk space and file permissions; write errors never trigger cross-account history deletion.

## Existing backend histories

When a verified account is read or recorded, the desktop adopts matching histories from local, iCloud and the currently configured Hub address into the shared account history. Original rows remain in the local database within normal retention; a transactional marker prevents repeat adoption. Overlapping observations go through the same source-conflict rules as new readings, rather than adding their consumption totals. Compressed intervals retain their coverage counts and gaps. Overlapping daily summaries older than detailed retention cannot be reconstructed safely and remain unknown.

Older stream IDs are one-way hashes and do not retain the Hub address. Histories from a former address can be discovered when that address is configured again; they cannot be recovered by guessing from account display names. Unverified identities continue to be backend-scoped. Remote devices supply current snapshots, not their full quota history.
